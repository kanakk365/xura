04 — The Demand Engine

AI data centers are the demand shock of the decade.

U.S. data-center power demand is set to more than double by 2027. Layer on electrification and reshoring, and a grid built for flat demand suddenly needs to grow ~25% by 2030 — while a 2,290 GW interconnection queue keeps new supply waiting nearly five years.

Big Tech committed $320B to AI/data-center buildout in 2025 — and BNEF pegs total data-center investment near $0.5T for the year.

The interconnection bottleneck LBNL 2025

  • 2,290 GW of generation + storage waiting in queue — roughly 2× total installed U.S. capacity.
  • 890 GW of that is battery storage alone.
  • 55 months median wait from request to commercial operation — up from 22 months in 2008.
  • Only 19% of 2000–2019 queue entrants ever reached operation — the queue is real, the bottleneck severe.
  • FERC Order 2023 is shifting to cluster-based "first-ready, first-served" processing.

06 — The Map

Two states hold the lion's share of the megawatts.

California and Texas combined accounted for 82% of utility-scale storage added in Q3 2025 alone. Texas's 2026 pipeline now outpaces California's — and is more than half the national plan.

California ~16.9 GW

  • Long the U.S. leader (~44% of capacity through mid-2025) — now neck-and-neck with Texas.
  • 13.9 GW utility-scale + 2.2 GW residential (200,000+ homes) + 849 MW commercial.
  • 3.4 GW of new additions planned for 2026 (~14% of national).

Texas ~13.9 GW

  • ERCOT entered 2026 with ~13.9 GW operational — surpassing California for the first time. Up from 3.6 GW in 2023.
  • 12.9 GW planned for 2026 — 53% of the national total.
  • Single projects topping 600 MW: Lunis Creek, Clear Fork Creek, Tehuacana Creek.

Arizona 3.2 GW

  • 3rd-largest 2026 planned additions (~13% of national).
  • A fast-rising Sun Belt market for utility-scale solar+storage.

08 — The Counter-Currents

Real headwinds temper the boom.

The thesis is strong, but not unqualified. Five forces could slow deployment — and each one rewards domestic, vertically integrated, interconnection-savvy operators.

Tariffs stack on imported cells

  • Non-EV lithium-ion batteries → 25% Section 301 duty by 2026.
  • Utility-scale systems with Chinese cell content now face 54%–100%+ combined import duties.
  • A cost headwind for 2026 deployment — but a tailwind for U.S. manufacturing.

Interconnection delays

  • 60%+ historical withdrawal rate — most queued projects never get built.
  • CAISO and PJM both suspended new applications in 2024 to clear backlog.
  • Much of the 890 GW storage queue is at risk of withdrawal.

IRA rollback (OBBBA, Jul 2025)

  • Consumer credits (25D rooftop, 30D EV, 25C efficiency) terminated.
  • Utility-scale 48E/45Y survive but must begin construction by Jul 4, 2026 (or be in service by end-2027).
  • New foreign-entity-of-concern rules bar 45X/45Y/48E credits for projects with Chinese-linked content.
  • Clean-investment pace fell 3 straight quarters post-enactment; $5B in manufacturing cancellations in Q2 2025.

Supply chain & resi contraction

  • China still dominates cathodes & anodes; U.S. factories cost 33% more to build, start at ~50% yields.
  • Residential storage projected to contract ~2% in 2026 after losing the 25D credit.
  • Cathode/anode supply remains China-sourced through 2030 even as assembly reshores.

Sources: Anza Renewables; TWAICE; LBNL; Holland & Knight; SEIA; Clean Investment Monitor Q2 2025; Wood Mackenzie Q1 2026 Monitor.
