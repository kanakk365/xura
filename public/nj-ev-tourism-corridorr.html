01 — Overview

Charging where people travel and stay.

The EV Tourism Corridor Charging Program offered substantial incentives to destinations along key travel routes — toll roads, interstates, and highways — that install publicly available EV chargers. Administered through New Jersey's Clean Energy Program (NJCEP) and delivered via Charge Up New Jersey, it targets the gap between where EV drivers go and where they can charge.

🎯

The goal

Enable confident EV travel and tourism across New Jersey by seeding fast, reliable charging at hotels, attractions, and destinations along designated corridors.

🏛️

Who runs it

New Jersey Board of Public Utilities (NJBPU) through New Jersey's Clean Energy Program; administered by the Center for Sustainable Energy under Charge Up New Jersey.

💰

How much

A $27,595,000 funding pool, paying up to 90% of charger costs — with 50% bonus incentives for sites in Overburdened Municipalities.

02 — Eligibility

Who could apply.

Open to destinations along key travel routes — with distance-to-corridor rules that differ by charger type.

Eligible applicants

  • Businesses — retail, attractions, destinations
  • Hotels — with dedicated Level 2 and DCFC allowances
  • Government & nonprofit entities

Distance to corridor

  • Level 2 — within 3 miles of an eligible corridor
  • DC fast — within 1 mile of an eligible corridor
  • Chargers must be publicly available

Per-site limits

  • Hotels — up to 4 incentivized chargers
  • Up to 2 of those may be DC fast chargers
  • DC fast — up to 2 per site

Eligible sites

Hotels

Overnight lodging establishments with 10 or more units, located within 3 miles of an eligible corridor.

Tourism locations

Sites within 1 mile of the nearest highway exit or intersection, with publicly available restrooms and one of: amusement, arts, culture, attractions, entertainment, history, or recreation.

Other commercial sites

Within 1 mile of a highway exit/intersection, with public restrooms and at least one amenity — convenience store, restaurant, or other retail store.

Site rules. Applicants could not receive incentives for more than one charging site within a 0.5-mile driving radius of another site they were applying for. Amenities and public restrooms had to be fully operational before incentive payments were released.
